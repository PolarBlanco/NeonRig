PLUG IN

  1. Plug the guitar into an audio interface or a USB guitar cable. On an interface: the instrument (Hi-Z) input, INST / Hi-Z on if it has the switch, and Direct Monitor OFF.
  2. Press START and allow microphone access. INPUT and OUTPUT on AUTO find the guitar and play out of the same box, or out of the computer's headphones/speakers for a cable with no output of its own. Plug things in or swap them any time; AUTO follows.
  3. Use wired headphones or speakers. Bluetooth adds too much delay to play along.
  4. Into your real amp: load Real Amp: Chorus + Reverb (CAB SIM off), take the interface's LINE OUT L (or 1) to the amp's input or FX return with a mono cable, Direct Monitor OFF, and start with the output low.
  5. Keep the input out of the red: the interface's input light should stay green when you dig in. Fine-tune with GAIN here.
  6. Watch the little 1 / 2 meters. Choose the channel your guitar lights up (AUTO does this for you).

KILL THE NOISE

  • Hold the strings quiet and press LEARN NOISE. It finds your mains hum (50 or 60 Hz) and sets the GATE just above your hiss.
  • Buzz that comes and goes as you turn is from single-coil pickups: face away from monitors and dimmers.
  • Crackles or dropouts are the computer, not the guitar: set BUF to SAFE (or MAX).
  • Static even when nothing is plugged in usually means a ground loop: try the laptop on battery (charger unplugged).

PLAY

  • Knobs: drag up/down, or scroll. Hold Shift for fine moves; double-click resets.
  • Footswitch (bottom of each pedal): click to engage or bypass. The LED shows the state.
  • Move pedals: drag them by the body. Reorder the signal: drag the chips in SIGNAL CHAIN.
  • ARRANGE lines everything up in chain order. + ADD PEDAL adds more (doubles are fine).
  • The board saves itself. Use SAVE to keep named presets.

LOOP

  • METRO runs the click at BPM (or click TAP in time). The space bar plays and stops the loop. Build a progression with + CHORD; click a chord to change, duplicate or delete it, and drag to reorder.
  • REC counts you in, records one pass of the progression as the chords light up, then loops it for you to play over.
  • REPEAT plays the loop a set number of times and then stops at the end of a pass (∞ = until STOP).
  • WAV downloads one pass of the loop as a file that loops seamlessly in other music apps.
  • Chords keep their audio when you reorder or duplicate them. The loop plays late against the click? Raise SYNC.

LATENCY

Browsers add some delay. Use Chrome or Edge, close other audio apps, and set your interface to 48 kHz in your system sound settings. The readout in the top bar is the browser's own estimate.
